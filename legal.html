Privacy Policy

Last Updated: October 15, 2026

1. Who we are

Atlas Church Solutions [legal entity name] ("Atlas," "we") provides a church operations platform at app.atlaschurchsolutions.com and a website at atlaschurchsolutions.com (together, the "Services"). This policy explains what we collect, why, who helps us process it, how long we keep it, and your choices.

2. Our two roles

  • For church data, the church is in charge. When a church uses Atlas, the church decides what goes in: staff profiles, member records, events, tasks, files, huddle notes and recordings. The church is the data controller; Atlas processes that data on the church's behalf and only to provide the Services (see our Data Processing Agreement).
  • For our own business data, Atlas is in charge. This covers church admin account and billing details, website visitors, and people who contact us.

3. What we collect

  • Account and billing: name, email, church name, role, password (stored hashed by our authentication provider), and billing details. Card numbers are collected and stored by Stripe; Atlas never sees the full number.
  • Church content: anything your church adds, including people records, departments, calendars, announcements, tasks, project boards, files, and huddles (agendas, notes, decisions, action items).
  • Huddle recordings and AI output (only if your church turns on Atlas AI): audio recordings, transcripts, AI-drafted summaries, suggested decisions and action items, and follow-up flags. See section 5.
  • Usage and device data: standard server logs kept by our hosting and database providers (such as IP address, browser type and the pages requested), used to keep the Services running and secure. Atlas does not use analytics, advertising or error-monitoring tools, and the app sets only the essential cookies needed to keep you signed in.
  • Website and inquiries: information you submit through our website forms (name, email, church, role, staff size, and any message you write). Our website does not use analytics or tracking cookies.

4. How we use it

To provide and secure the Services; to process subscriptions and payments; to send account, security, invitation and notification emails; to respond to support requests; to follow up on website inquiries; and to improve the Services using aggregated information that doesn't identify individuals or churches. We do not sell personal data, and we do not use church content to train AI models.

5. Atlas AI and huddle recordings

Atlas AI is off unless a church admin accepts the Atlas AI Terms. When it's on:

  • Transcription: huddle audio is sent to OpenAI to be transcribed.
  • Summaries: transcripts and huddle notes are sent to Anthropic to draft a summary, decisions, action items and follow-up flags. Huddle summaries are only ever processed by Anthropic.
  • Backup processing for some features: for certain other AI features, if Anthropic can't be used (for example, when a church's AI allowance runs out), Atlas may send the request to OpenAI instead. Features that can surface sensitive pastoral content, including huddle summaries, never do this; they stop instead.
  • Provider terms: both providers process this data under their business/API terms. As of October 14, 2026, both state that they do not train their models on API data by default, and that they keep API inputs and outputs for a limited period (generally up to 30 days) to provide the service and detect abuse, longer only where required by law or, for Anthropic, where content is flagged under its usage policy.
  • Who sees it inside the church: recordings, transcripts and draft summaries are visible to the huddle organizer and church admins. Other attendees see a summary only after the organizer accepts it. Follow-up flags are visible to the huddle organizer only. Church admins can export all of the church's data, including transcripts and accepted summaries from every huddle, private ones included. Exports don't include audio recordings or follow-up flags.
  • Follow-up flags are prompts for pastoral attention, not a diagnosis, a crisis service, or a guarantee that anything concerning will be noticed.
  • Consent: the person who starts a recording is responsible for telling those present. Atlas shows a recording indicator while recording.
  • Turning it off: an admin can turn AI off at any time; after that, nothing new is sent to AI providers.

6. Who we share it with

We share personal data only with service providers that help us run Atlas ("subprocessors"), under contracts that limit their use to providing their service to us; when required by law; to protect the safety of people or the Services; or as part of a merger or sale of the business, with notice. Our current subprocessors are:

  • Supabase: database, authentication, file and recording storage. All Service data. United States (AWS us-west-2).
  • Vercel: application and website hosting. Requests and logs. United States.
  • Stripe: payments and subscriptions. Billing contact and payment details. United States.
  • OpenAI: huddle audio transcription, and backup text processing for some AI features (AI on only). United States.
  • Anthropic: huddle summaries (AI on only). Transcripts, huddle notes, attendee names. United States.
  • Resend: transactional and notification email. Name, email, message content. United States.
  • HubSpot: website inquiry and contact forms (marketing website only, not the app). Form submissions. United States.

The full list, with links to each provider's privacy policy, lives on our sub-processors page. We'll give churches notice before adding a new one, as described in the Data Processing Agreement.

7. How long we keep it

  • Huddle recordings: deleted automatically after the plan's retention period (30 days on Workspace), or sooner if the organizer or an admin deletes them.
  • Transcripts: deleted together with their recording, on the same schedule.
  • Accepted summaries, decisions and action items: kept until the church deletes them.
  • Follow-up flags: deleted 90 days after the huddle.
  • Other church content: kept while the church's account is active. After an account closes, the church has 30 days to export its data; after that it is permanently deleted from our systems, and from backups within 30 days.
  • Billing records: kept as long as tax and accounting law requires.
  • AI providers: see section 5.

8. Security

We encrypt data in transit (TLS) and at rest, keep recordings in private storage reachable only through our servers, restrict access by role, and limit Atlas staff access to what's needed to support you. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your data, we'll notify affected churches without undue delay and as the law requires.

9. Children

Atlas is for church staff and isn't directed to children. Churches may store information about children in their ministries; the church is responsible for having the right to do so, including any parental consent the law requires. We use that information only to provide the Services to the church. Churches should avoid recording huddles in ways that capture children's sensitive information unless they have a clear need and the appropriate consent.

10. Your rights and choices

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or export your personal data, and to object to or limit certain processing. We don't sell or "share" personal data for cross-context advertising.

  • Church admins and website visitors: email privacy@atlaschurchsolutions.com. We'll respond within 45 days.
  • Church members and staff: your church controls your data. Ask your church first; we'll help them respond.

Admins can export the church's data from Settings at any time.

11. Where data is processed

Atlas and its subprocessors process data in the United States. If you use Atlas from outside the U.S., your data will be transferred to the U.S.

12. Changes

We'll post updates here and change the date above. For material changes, we'll email church admins at least 30 days before they take effect. Changes to how Atlas AI handles data also require an admin to accept the updated AI Terms before AI continues.

13. Contact

privacy@atlaschurchsolutions.com · [mailing address]

Data Processing Agreement

Last Updated: October 15, 2026

1. Scope and purpose

This Data Processing Agreement ("DPA") is between Atlas Church Solutions ("Atlas") and the church or organization using the Services ("Customer"). It applies whenever the Customer is a controller of personal data and Atlas processes that data on the Customer's behalf to provide the Services. By accepting the Terms of Service, the Customer enters into this DPA. "Personal Data" has the meaning given in applicable data protection laws, including the CCPA and GDPR.

2. Description of the processing

  • Subject matter: providing the Atlas church operations platform to the Customer.
  • Duration: the Customer's subscription, plus the 30-day export window after it ends.
  • Nature and purpose: storing, organizing, displaying and backing up the Customer's content so its staff can use it; sending notification emails; and, when the Customer turns on Atlas AI, transcribing huddle recordings and drafting summaries.
  • Types of data: names, contact details, roles and departments; events, tasks, announcements, project boards and files; huddle agendas, notes, decisions and action items; and, with Atlas AI on, huddle audio recordings, transcripts, AI-drafted summaries and follow-up flags.
  • Categories of people: the Customer's staff, volunteers, members, attendees and others the Customer chooses to record in Atlas.

3. Subprocessors

Atlas uses the subprocessors listed on our sub-processors page, and the Customer authorizes them by accepting these terms. Atlas will tell Customers at least 30 days before a new or replacement subprocessor begins processing Personal Data, so they can object. Customers may object in writing to privacy@atlaschurchsolutions.com within 30 days of the notice. If Atlas can't accommodate an objection, the Customer may end the affected Services and receive a pro-rated refund of any prepaid, unused fees.

4. Atlas's obligations and confidentiality

Atlas processes Personal Data only on the Customer's documented instructions, which include the Terms of Service, this DPA and the Customer's use of the Services. Everyone at Atlas who can access Personal Data is bound by confidentiality obligations.

5. Helping with data subject requests

Atlas will help the Customer respond to requests from individuals to access, correct, delete, restrict or export their Personal Data, using the tools in the Services (including admin export) and reasonable additional help. If an individual contacts Atlas directly, we'll pass the request to the Customer and follow the Customer's instructions.

6. Security

Atlas encrypts Personal Data in transit (TLS) and at rest, keeps huddle recordings in private storage reachable only through Atlas's servers, restricts access by role inside the Services, and limits Atlas staff access to what's needed to operate and support the Services. Atlas will keep these measures in place and improve them over time.

7. Breach notification

If Atlas becomes aware of a Personal Data breach affecting the Customer's data, Atlas will notify the Customer without undue delay and no later than 72 hours after becoming aware of it, with enough information for the Customer to notify authorities and affected individuals as required.

8. Where data is processed

Atlas and its subprocessors process Personal Data in the United States. Where the law requires safeguards for transferring Personal Data from another country to the U.S., Atlas will rely on appropriate mechanisms such as Standard Contractual Clauses.

9. Return or deletion of data

When the Services end, the Customer has 30 days to export its content. After that, Atlas permanently deletes the Customer's Personal Data from its systems, and from backups within a further 30 days, unless the law requires Atlas to keep it. Atlas will confirm deletion in writing on request.

10. Showing compliance

Atlas will give the Customer the information reasonably needed to show that it is meeting this DPA, including answers to a security questionnaire. Where the law gives the Customer a right to audit, Atlas will cooperate with reasonable, pre-arranged audits that don't disrupt the Services or expose other customers' data.

Cookie Policy

Last Updated: October 15, 2026

1. What cookies are

Cookies are small text files stored on your device by a website or app. Atlas uses them only to keep the Services working, and the short list below is everything we set.

2. Our website (atlaschurchsolutions.com)

The website sets no tracking or analytics cookies and shows no cookie banner, because there is nothing to consent to. When you submit a form, it goes directly to HubSpot, which receives what you entered and your IP address. The website also loads fonts from Google Fonts and an animation library (GSAP) from cdnjs, a content delivery network run by Cloudflare; those providers see your IP address when the files load, as with any website resource.

3. The Atlas app (app.atlaschurchsolutions.com)

The app sets essential cookies only:

  • Supabase authentication cookies (names beginning sb-): keep you signed in. Cleared when you sign out, or after the session expires.
  • atlas_pending_email: remembers the email address you used to start signing up, for one day, so the next step can find your account.

We don't use analytics, advertising or error-monitoring cookies in the app.

4. Controlling cookies

You can block or delete cookies in your browser's settings. If you block the essential cookies above, you won't be able to stay signed in to the app. We don't currently respond to "Do Not Track" signals, because we don't track.

5. Changes and contact

If we add a cookie, we'll list it here and update the date at the top of this section. Questions: privacy@atlaschurchsolutions.com.
